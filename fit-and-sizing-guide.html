Fit & Sizing Guide

How to measure yourself once, read any size chart, and stop guessing between UK, EU and US labels.

There is no legal definition of a size 10. Every brand builds its patterns on its own fit model, then adds its own ease. That is why the only numbers worth trusting are your own, written down and kept on your phone.

The five measurements to take

Stand relaxed, wear the underwear you would normally wear, and keep the tape level and snug without pulling.

  • Bust or chest: around the fullest part, under the arms.
  • Waist: the narrowest point, usually just above the navel.
  • Hip: around the fullest part, roughly 20–22 cm below the waist.
  • Inside leg: from the crotch seam to where you want the hem to fall, in the shoes you will wear.
  • Shoulder: across the back from the point of one shoulder to the other.

Ease: the number nobody prints

Ease is the difference between your body and the garment. A fitted shirt might add 5–8 cm at the chest; a relaxed overshirt adds 20 cm or more. Two shirts labelled the same size can differ by a full hand span simply because one is drafted close and the other loose. When a brand publishes garment measurements rather than body measurements, that is ease made visible, and it is the most useful chart you can be given.

Reading across countries

US, UK, EU and IT sizes map onto each other roughly: a US 6 is usually a UK 10, an EU 38 and an IT 42. The gap between systems is consistent enough to be useful and loose enough that you should still check the centimetres. Our Size Converter gives the conversion and the body measurement each size is drafted for.

Why vanity sizing exists

Over the last fifty years the body measurements attached to a given size number have crept upwards, so that the same label now covers a larger body than it once did. It is a marketing decision, not a standard. The practical consequence is simple: compare centimetres, never numbers, and never between decades.

What fits and what can be fixed

Hems, waists, sleeve length and taking in a side seam are all routine alterations. Shoulders, armholes and anything across the chest of a tailored jacket are expensive or impossible. So buy for the part that cannot be changed and have the rest adjusted.

Fabric changes fit

The same pattern in a crisp poplin and a soft viscose will not fit the same way. Woven cloth with no stretch needs more ease to move in; a knit or a blend with elastane can be cut closer because the cloth gives. If you are between sizes, size up in a stiff woven and size down in a stretchy knit. The Blend Builder will tell you which you are dealing with.
